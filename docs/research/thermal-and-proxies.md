# Sustained throughput, latency predictors and cheap sensitivity proxies: who already does what Ising-Edge does

Scope (30 Sep 2026). This note checks the prior work behind two candidate directions and behind the planner's novelty claims. D2 would replace the per-layer WikiText-2 perplexity (PPL) rows with a forward-only proxy such as KL(dense‖compressed) on 64 × 256 tokens, accepted only at Spearman ρ ≥ 0.9 against PPL. D4 would plan for sustained (thermally throttled) throughput instead of cold-start numbers. It answers four questions: how much on-device LLM throughput drops over minutes on Snapdragon, per compute unit, and whether anyone plans placement for it; what transferable latency predictors achieve compared with SnapTwin; what rank correlation cheap sensitivity proxies reach against PPL and task accuracy; and whether a joint compression + quantisation planner already covers low-rank × bits × placement. Every seed link was opened on arxiv.org (abstract page plus the HTML or PDF text), and follow-up papers found by search were opened the same way unless tagged [unsure]. Nothing here is a measurement of ours. Device labels are the papers' own, and no phone number is presented as a tablet or PC result.

## Findings

1. **Seed links: all seven exist, and five differ from how they were summarised to us.** 2603.23640 tests only the Adreno GPU on its Snapdragon phone; the "NPU" in its title is a Hailo-10H on a Raspberry Pi 5. 2609.30270 (HybridInfer) routes queries between device, edge and cloud tiers, not between CPU/GPU/NPU. 2607.21602 has no Snapdragon or other Qualcomm device. 2604.13440 studies only SSM and hybrid SSM-Transformer models and reports Kendall τ, not Spearman ρ. 2609.34866 reports no rank correlation against PPL or accuracy. AutoQRA's "rank" is the LoRA adapter rank used in fine-tuning, not low-rank compression of the weights. 2606.07819 jointly chooses structured pruning and bits, not low rank and bits. [verified]
2. **GPU, Snapdragon 8 Gen 3, sustained (2603.23640 v2).** The run used Qwen 2.5 1.5B on a Galaxy S24 Ultra (Snapdragon 8 Gen 3, Adreno 750) with MLC-LLM 0.1.0 and `q4f16_2` (Tables 1–2), a fixed 258-token prompt and 20 warm iterations. Decode peaks at 12.21 tok/s during a 1,000 MHz DVFS boost (iterations 4–5). From iteration 8 onward it averages 10.38 ± 0.44 tok/s at 720–770 MHz (§4.4). The abstract and §5.1 call this a ~15 % drop, while §4.4 also quotes "a 22% degradation from peak". Table 8 over 20 iterations gives a mean of 10.83, a minimum of 9.55 and a maximum of 12.21 tok/s, with a mean decode time of 59,908 ms per iteration. [verified] Arithmetic: 1 − 10.38/12.21 = 15.0 % and 1 − 9.55/12.21 = 21.8 %, so the 22 % matches the worst iteration. Twenty iterations of ~60 s decode are about 20 min of decode. [inferred]
3. **The same phone failed outright in v1 of that paper.** In v1 (24 Mar 2026) the S24 Ultra completed 5 valid iterations at 9.93 ± 0.79 tok/s. At iteration 6 it hit "a hard OS-enforced thermal failure" (GPU 78.3 °C, CPU 73.8 °C) and completed no further iterations (§4.4, Table 8). v2 (7 Jun 2026) set a 2,048-token context and a 128-token MLC prefill chunk and kept the display off "to keep the device within the governor's sustained budget across 20 iterations" (§5.4). v2 notes that the smaller chunk inflates prefill latency. The arXiv abstract page still shows v1's sentence, "the S24 Ultra suffers a hard OS-enforced GPU frequency floor that terminates inference entirely". Which of the two outcomes a run gets depends on the runtime configuration, not only on the chip. [verified]
4. **Paper-level conclusion (2603.23640 v2, §5.1).** The paper states that "the operative ceiling for an always-on workload is the plateau throughput rather than the peak". The iPhone 16 Pro (not Snapdragon) falls from 40.49 to 23.67 ± 0.28 tok/s in its Hot state, a 41.5 % drop (§4.3). The paper benchmarks this but does not plan anything from it. [verified]
5. **GPU, Snapdragon 8 Elite, sustained (2609.30270, HybridInfer).** The on-device tier is a Galaxy S25+ (Snapdragon 8 Elite, Adreno 830) running Llama 3.2 3B `q4f16_0` through MLC-LLM over OpenCL. §IV-A says it "decodes at roughly 14 to 24 tokens/s on the Adreno 830 when the device is cool, falling toward 4 to 13 tokens/s as it throttles under sustained load"; the same section also quotes ≈14–20 tok/s when cool. In a continuous run the app "crashed or was killed after roughly 4 to 12 consecutive on-device queries", and 22 of 40 long prompts wedged past a 240 s cap (§IV-B, Fig. 2). The same failures occur "when the device is cool (thermal headroom near 0.6, no skin throttling)", so the instability comes partly from the MLC/OpenCL stack and not only from heat. [verified] If the range ends are paired, the drop is 46–71 % (1 − 13/24, 1 − 4/14). [inferred]
6. **HybridInfer plans tiers, not units.** It is a Q-learning router with 15 states: 5 bins of Android `getThermalHeadroom` × 3 query-complexity bins. The router chooses between on-device Llama 3.2 3B, edge Llama 3.1 8B with retrieval, and cloud GPT-4o. The reward includes a thermal penalty and a locality bonus, and the paper claims to be the first to use on-device thermal headroom "to select among LLM inference tiers". It never chooses CPU, GPU or NPU on the phone. [verified]
7. **CPU, Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 (2410.03613 v5, §V-D).** The runs use llama.cpp on the CPU of a Xiaomi 14 Pro. There, "throughput for both prefill and decode initially rises, then decreases, and finally stabilizes". Snapdragon SoCs show "a more aggressive DVFS policy", and "by the 99th inference round, the frequency is nearly halved" (Fig. 13). On the MLC GPU path, inference speed stays stable, with the GPU clock "generally fixed at 815 MHz" on a Xiaomi Pad 6 Pro (Fig. 16). The text gives no tok/s percentage; the curves are only in the figures. [verified]
8. **NPU vs CPU, Snapdragon 8 Elite (2606.27906, "Phase Matters", §4.1).** The workload is FastVLM-0.5B (a VLM, not an LLM), with QNN on the NPU and LiteRT/XNNPACK FP16 on the CPU, on SM8750 at 23 °C on AC power. Over 100 back-to-back runs the NPU averages 40.78 ± 3.03 °C and the CPU 51.25 ± 3.62 °C. Under "15 minutes of continuous VLM inference", the paper says "CPU paths reach 82 °C and trigger governor-driven clock reduction, while NPU paths stabilize at 66 °C and remain below the thermal throttle trip point (85 °C skin per Qualcomm thermal HAL configuration)". The paper gives no tok/s-over-time curve. [verified]
9. **No sustained Hexagon LLM curve exists in what we read.** 2607.05475 ("Is Your NPU Ready for LLMs?") cools devices "to below 28 °C to avoid thermal throttling" before every run (§3.4), so its numbers are cold. EnerInfer (2606.23001) shows that NPUs also hit skin limits: on Huawei phones (Mate70 Pro, Pura 80; NPU only), default maximum clocks produce 690 tokens in 40 s before the 42 °C back-shell threshold. Its thermal controller produces 27.9 % more tokens and lasts 32.1 % longer (§6.3, Fig. 14). EnerInfer changes only NPU and DDR frequencies and never moves work between units. [verified] In the searches for this note we found no Snapdragon Hexagon LLM decode tok/s trace over 5–10+ min. [inferred]
10. **Nobody we found plans LLM placement for sustained throughput.** The closest works are HybridInfer (tiers, finding 6), EnerInfer (DVFS, finding 9) and, for DNNs, Tan & Cao (IEEE Trans. Mobile Comput., 2024, doi 10.1109/TMC.2024.3379501). HybridInfer cites Tan & Cao as "thermal-aware scheduling between the GPU and NPU for on-device DNNs" [verified]. Their abstract, seen only in search results, describes a heuristic and a DRL scheduler that trade processing time against accuracy (the NPU uses lower precision) while avoiding overheating; the ACM and PSU copies returned 403 and a certificate error, so this is unconfirmed [unsure]. As far as this search reaches, joint placement × compression × bits for sustained LLM throughput is unclaimed. [inferred]
11. **Transferable latency prediction (2607.21602, Tu et al.): what and where.** It predicts per-request total, prefill and decode latency. Its inputs are static descriptors (device, accelerator, memory, model family and size, quantisation format, runtime backend, input and generation length) fused by a gated network with runtime telemetry (utilisation, frequency, temperature, memory, power) "collected during profiling" (§3.3). The data are 515 request records (Table 4) on Pixel 8, Pixel 8 Pro, Jetson Nano, Orange Pi 5 Pro and an RTX 3090-class GPU, with SmolLM2 135M/360M/1.7B, TinyLlama-1.1B and OPT-350M. It uses the predictions for Pareto screening of candidate models (§3.7–3.8). [verified]
12. **Transferable latency prediction: errors.** Within one device, Table 5 (full model) reports MAPE on Pixel 8 of 35.57 % prefill, 10.76 % decode and 11.87 % total; on Pixel 8 Pro, 2.33 / 4.62 / 6.12 %. Cross-device transfer is in Table 6 (train/calibration/test split in brackets). Pixel 8 Pro → Pixel 8 with 6 target calibration samples (204/6/50) gives 23.92 / 20.99 / 18.23 % (total R² 0.940). Pixel 8 → Pixel 8 Pro with 20 samples (56/20/184) gives 56.45 / 32.18 / 32.38 %. Before calibration, decode MAPE is 443.61 % and 76.38 %. [verified]
13. **The closest prior work to SnapTwin (2609.14864, Qiu et al., 14 Sep 2026).** It predicts decode throughput from GGUF metadata as T̂_d = η_{h,q} · B_h / D(c), with a fitted efficiency per host and quantisation format. That is the same roofline form as SnapTwin's 1/a_w. The data are 318 phase–depth measurements from 53 host–file configurations on two Apple M4 Max machines and one RTX 5080. Held-out decode MAPE is 13.1 / 14.4 / 36.1 % and held-out prefill MAPE 18.7 / 22.2 / 108.2 % (Table 1); leave-one-host-out decode MAPE is 11.6–36.0 %. The authors conclude that "fitted efficiencies are not universal" (§5). There are no mobile, Snapdragon, NPU or thermal data, and the paper states "No GPU clock, power, or spill telemetry was recorded". [verified]
14. **NPU latency prediction exists only for datacenter NPUs.** LENS (2606.18042) predicts NPU LLM latency at a 2.15 % mean error from two end-to-end measurements per bucket. It was tested only on AWS Inferentia2 and Google TPU v4/v5e/v6e in 4-chip configurations (§5.1). [verified]
15. **KL lens (2604.13440, Kong, Pandey, Ponzina, Rosing; 15 Apr 2026): what it measures.** Each layer is quantised alone to INT8, and the ground truth is the resulting ΔPPL on WikiText-2 (§5.2). Table 2 gives Kendall τ between the proxy ranking and the ΔPPL ranking for "KL_student→teacher": 0.8419 (Mamba-130M), 0.7936 (Mamba-380M), 0.8327 (Mamba-1.4B), 0.8078 (Mamba2-130M), 0.6646 (Hymba) and 0.8060 (Zamba), mean 0.7911. SQNR reaches a mean of 0.7111, the reverse KL −0.1275 and ΔCE −0.0645. p-values are in Table 3. On Intel Lunar Lake (OpenVINO), the KL-guided Mamba-1.4B configuration p05 runs at 178 ms, against 196 ms for uniform INT8 and 190 ms for INT4 (§7.2). [verified]
16. **KL lens: what it does not show.** Algorithm 1 takes the KL and the PPL from the same `EVALUATE(·, D)` pass over the same dataset. The paper gives no sample, sequence or token count for the sensitivity passes, so it shows that KL ranks like ΔPPL on the same data, not that KL on fewer tokens does. Its direction labels are also inconsistent. §4.1 defines KL(P‖Q) = E_P[log P − log Q] with P the original model. §4.2 says the good direction "strongly penalizes probability mass that the student assigns to tokens the teacher considers unlikely", while "the reverse direction ... shows a weak negative correlation (τ ≈ −0.14)". [verified] The §4.2 description fits KL(quantised‖original), which is the reverse of our planned KL(dense‖compressed), but the paper's own formula suggests the opposite; both directions must be tested. [inferred]
17. **Converting the KL lens τ to our Spearman bar.** Under a Gaussian copula, ρ_s = (6/π)·arcsin(sin(πτ/2)/2). Mean τ 0.7911 gives ρ_s ≈ 0.94. Per model the values are 0.97, 0.94, 0.96, 0.95, 0.85 (Hymba) and 0.95, so one of six models falls below 0.9. The distribution-free Daniels bound guarantees only ρ_s ≥ (3τ − 1)/2 = 0.50–0.76. [inferred]
18. **JAB (2609.34866, HosseinpourFardi et al., 28 Sep 2026; GPT-2 small and Mistral-7B, widths {2, 3, 4, 8, 16}).** The paper reports no rank correlation between any sensitivity estimate and PPL or accuracy. Its only correlation is a Pearson r = 0.9995 between Hessian traces calibrated on WikiText-2 and on C4 (App. D). Its "oracle" is our D2 proxy: "each (i, b) pair's end-to-end logit KL by quantizing only layer i (≈ 73 model loads)" (§2.4). On GPT-2's MLP at 3 bits, the Hessian criterion (120.70 PPL) is worse than uniform (53.71), while the KL oracle improves on it (46.15) (Table 2). Calibration is "10 Hutchinson probes on 2 calibration batches per layer", and Mistral-7B PPL is measured on 32 WikiText-2 windows of 512 tokens (§3, App. C). [verified]
19. **JAB: matrix role beats sensitivity, and local objectives mislead.** A role-aware offset rule with no sensitivity estimate beats JAB on full Mistral-7B: 6.933 against 7.158 PPL at 4.5 bits per parameter (FP16 6.643; abstract), and 6.970 against 7.250 in Table 4. The paper finds that the oracle's allocation "has no depth structure at all, only a role split" (§6.3). Refinement improved a block-local objective 4.6× while PPL rose from 7.373 to 239.52 (Table 5), and "the additive objective ... cannot see the low-bit cliff" (App. E). [verified]
20. **Activation error vs weight error (2605.30836, Khilar).** On Pythia-1.4B, with W_O compressed at d/D = 1/4 and calibration on 256 WikiText-2 sequences × 2048 tokens (§7.1), "the Spearman rank correlation between per-layer activation error and perplexity is +0.97, while the rank correlation between weight-Frobenius reconstruction and perplexity is −0.43" (§7.4). This is over "all 9 configurations" of method, λ and anchor in the PDF text; the HTML rendering we fetched reads "99". The ρ is across method configurations on one weight class, not a ranking of layers. [verified]
21. **Weight-only spectral proxies predict accuracy but not PPL (2604.18085, Xu).** The study covers Qwen3 0.6B–14B and Gemma3 270M–27B under vanilla SVD, two ASVD variants and SVD-LLM. Compression ratio × stable rank predicts accuracy loss with leave-one-out Pearson 0.890 (attention) and 0.839 (MLP), but "the best overall perplexity leave-one-out correlation is only 0.093, yet layer-specific analysis reaches 0.736 for MLP". The paper also states that "Layer type matters more than compression method" (§1). [verified]
22. **PPL rankings need not track reasoning (2607.00908, Wang et al.).** The paper calls this the "Perplexity Illusion". Per-layer W3 PPL sensitivity (WikiText-2) and GSM8K conditional-loss sensitivity reach Kendall τ = −0.08 (p = 0.53) on LLaMA-3-8B (Table 1), and the top-8 layers overlap on only 2 of 8 (App. B.5); calibration uses 128 samples. Qwen2.5-7B is also studied, and the abstract states τ ≈ 0 overall. [verified]
23. **AutoQRA (2602.22268, Zhou et al., 25 Feb 2026).** It chooses a per-layer (bit-width, LoRA rank) configuration for quantised fine-tuning under a strict memory budget (§3.1, Eq. 1). Phase I runs a multi-fidelity evolutionary search with NSGA-II and constrained domination, warm-started from layer-importance priors, with 25 low-fidelity and 3 high-fidelity evaluations (§3.2, §4.1, App. C.1). Phase II is trust-region Bayesian optimisation. The coupling it models is bits × LoRA rank inside training ("bit width and LoRA rank interact"). Cross-layer effects enter only because whole configurations are scored; there are no pairwise terms. It has no low-rank compression of the base weights and no latency or placement model. Models: LLaMA-3.1-8B, LLaMA-3.2-3B, Qwen-2.5-7B and Qwen-2.5-3B. [verified]
24. **Joint pruning and mixed precision (2606.07819, La et al., 5 Jun 2026).** Structured channel pruning masks and per-layer mixed-precision PTQ are learned together by a hypernetwork with Gumbel-softmax + STE. The loss is end-to-end cross-entropy plus a log-ratio budget regulariser (Eqs. 6–7). Cross-layer error propagation is therefore in the loss, not in explicit pairwise terms. It is evaluated on Llama-2/3/3.1/3.2, Mistral-7B and Qwen3-8B, with GPU kernels on an NVIDIA L40. It reports "up to 21%" lower WikiText PPL than weight-activation baselines at 1–3 bits. It uses no low-rank compression and no CPU/GPU/NPU placement. [verified]
25. **Joint rank × bits and pairwise bit allocation already exist.** MLoRQ (2507.09616, BMVC 2025) assigns a rank and a bit-width to each layer under a memory constraint in two stages (intra-layer candidates, then inter-layer allocation), for vision transformers (abstract only; its inter-layer solver is [unsure]). UniQL (2512.03383) combines fixed 4-bit GPTQ with layer-wise pruning ratios from Block Influence scores on an A6000 and a Jetson Orin Nano, with no placement (§3.3, §3.4, §4.1). CoopQ (2509.15455) estimates inter-layer interactions with Shapley values and solves 2- or 4-bit allocation as a binary quadratic program on Llama-3, Gemma-2 and Qwen-3 (abstract). None of them chooses a compute unit or uses a device latency model. [verified]
26. **Placement without compression.** HeteroMosaic (2607.12839) co-optimises scheduling and device allocation across CPU, iGPU and NPU on three AMD Ryzen AI platforms: 1.73× over an iGPU baseline, 1.78× over an NPU baseline and up to 45.3 % less energy. ATSInfer (2607.10183) places tensors across CPU and GPU with load-aware dynamic transfer on consumer PCs. Neither compresses, and neither is thermal (from the abstracts). [verified]
27. **Answer to the main question.** Pieces of Ising-Edge exist separately: rank × bits (MLoRQ), bits × LoRA rank (AutoQRA), pruning × bits (2606.07819), pairwise quadratic bit allocation (CoopQ, and the CLADO and CASA already cited in PROPOSAL-v3), placement (HeteroMosaic, ATSInfer, HeteroInfer), a GGUF roofline predictor (2609.14864), and thermal tier routing and DVFS control (HybridInfer, EnerInfer). We found no work that chooses low rank × bits × CPU/GPU/NPU placement jointly with pairwise accuracy terms on Snapdragon, sustained or not; this rests on these searches finding nothing, not on proof. [inferred]

## Contradictions with our results

- **SnapTwin models peak, not sustained, throughput.** It is fitted on Qualcomm's GenieX rows, each a (context − 128)-token prompt plus 128 generated tokens (`docs/TWIN.md`). These are short runs whose thermal state is not reported [unsure], so every SnapTwin prediction is a cold or peak estimate. Published Snapdragon GPU plateaus sit 15 % below peak (finding 2), and HybridInfer's ranges imply up to 46–71 % (finding 5). The sustained effect is therefore at least comparable to SnapTwin's own 21 % CPU/GPU decode transfer error, so D4 cannot be validated from published rows; it needs our own device runs. [inferred]
- **SnapTwin's decode law is not new, and its errors are in line with prior work.** The GGUF-metadata roofline (finding 13) has the same form, with 11.6–36.0 % leave-one-host-out error against our 21 % median phone → PC CPU/GPU decode. Our prefill failure (47–61 %) is echoed by its 108.2 % prefill error on the RTX. Tu et al. need 6–20 target samples and still report 18–32 % total MAPE between two Pixel phones (finding 12); SnapTwin uses zero target rows. Our figures are medians and theirs are means, so these are magnitudes, not a ranking. [inferred]
- **Per-layer proxies cannot price plans.** The KL lens allocates bits by a per-layer KL threshold, which is additive. Our data are super-additive: 24 MLPs at keep 0.8 cost +276 % measured against +58 % summed from single layers (`docs/PROPOSAL.md`), matching JAB's statement that an additive objective "cannot see the low-bit cliff" (finding 19). A KL table would replace only the single-layer rows. The pair terms and the end-to-end check of each chosen plan stay. [inferred]
- **D2 as specified barely saves tokens.** The single-layer PPL rows already use 40 WikiText-2 chunks at ctx 512, which is 20,480 tokens processed per row (`docs/PROPOSAL.md`, `docs/LOG.md`). The proposed KL uses 64 × 256 = 16,384 tokens, and both need one forward pass of the compressed model per option. D2 saves compute only if KL reaches ρ ≥ 0.9 on far fewer tokens, or if its tooling is cheaper than patching GGUFs; no paper above tests either. The JAB oracle already ran end-to-end KL at one pass per option. [inferred]
- **Our damage has a depth structure; JAB's oracle had none.** JAB's KL oracle allocated bits "by role, not depth" (finding 19), but our low-rank damage is strongly depth-dependent: layer 27 costs +24.7 % at keep 0.8, and the layers 0+1 pair interacts at +10.0 pp. The knob (rank vs bits) and model differ, so this is not a direct contradiction. It does warn that planning at block level (attention vs MLP) can miss per-matrix role effects (gate/up/down). [inferred]
- **Weight-space objectives are the wrong target (consistent).** Activation-aware SVD halving the median single-layer cost (+2.68 % vs +5.85 %) and avoiding the data-free collapse agrees with 2605.30836 (activation error ρ +0.97, weight Frobenius −0.43) and with 2604.18085 (weight-only statistics predict PPL at r 0.093 overall). [inferred]
- **The PPL target itself may be off for agents.** The planner's accuracy term is WikiText-2 PPL only, yet per-layer PPL sensitivity and reasoning sensitivity reach τ = −0.08 on LLaMA-3-8B (finding 22). A proxy that matches PPL at ρ ≥ 0.9 inherits this gap. [inferred]

## Recommendation

1. KL proxy (D2): supported only by analogy. The one direct test (2604.13440: six SSM/hybrid models, per-layer INT8) finds Kendall τ 0.66–0.84 (mean 0.79) against ΔPPL, about Spearman 0.85–0.97 (mean ≈ 0.94) under a Gaussian-copula conversion, but on the same data as the PPL, and the wrong KL direction scores τ ≈ −0.13. No paper tests KL for low-rank SVD, on Qwen3, or on 16k tokens, so D2 ships only if our own ρ ≥ 0.9 holds on the existing Qwen3-0.6B single-layer rows.
2. Thermal-sustained planning (D4): we found no paper that plans LLM unit placement (let alone compression) for throttled throughput. HybridInfer routes tiers, EnerInfer sets NPU/DDR clocks on Huawei phones, and Tan & Cao (TMC 2024, unopened) schedule GPU/NPU for DNNs. Expect Adreno decode to settle about 15 % below peak (22 % worst) over 20 runs of ~60 s on Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 (2603.23640 v2 §4.4, Table 8), 14–24 → 4–13 tok/s with crashes on Snapdragon 8 Elite (2609.30270 §IV), CPU clocks near halved (2410.03613 Fig. 13), and the NPU unthrottled at 66 °C against the CPU's 82 °C (2606.27906 §4.1). No sustained Hexagon LLM tok/s curve exists.
3. Joint compression + quantisation is already done, but without placement: MLoRQ (rank × bits per layer, ViTs, two-stage), AutoQRA (bits × LoRA rank, NSGA-II multi-fidelity + trust-region BO), 2606.07819 (pruning × bits, Gumbel-softmax hypernetwork on end-to-end loss) and UniQL (4-bit + BI-sorted ranks). CoopQ already solves pairwise-interaction bit allocation as a binary quadratic program for Llama-3, Gemma-2 and Qwen-3.
4. Do not claim: the first joint compression + quantisation planner; the first pairwise or QUBO cross-layer allocator for LLMs (CoopQ, CLADO, CASA); a new llama.cpp performance-model form (2609.14864); that KL or PPL rankings predict agent or reasoning accuracy (τ −0.08, 2607.00908); that HybridInfer or 2603.23640 measured a Hexagon NPU; or any sustained phone, tablet or PC number we have not measured ourselves.
5. Kill criterion: drop D2 if neither KL direction reaches Spearman ρ ≥ 0.9 against ΔPPL on the 28 activation-aware keep-0.8 single-layer rows at 64 × 256 tokens. Also drop it if the bootstrap 95 % lower bound is below 0.8, since at n = 28 an observed 0.9 carries a CI of about 0.79–0.95 (Fisher z, variance 1.06/(n − 3)), or if a KL-chosen plan measures worse PPL than the PPL-chosen plan at matched bytes. Drop D4 if the Pad 4's 10-minute continuous decode stays within 10 % of cold on every unit and never changes the unit ranking.
