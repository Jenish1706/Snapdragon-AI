# Closed-loop surrogate planning (FMQA, BOCS and relatives): what the literature says about direction D1

Scope (30 Sep 2026). D1 would replace the one-shot pairwise planner with a closed measure → fit → solve loop over the 24 MLP blocks of Qwen3-0.6B (layers 2–25) × {dense, keep 0.9, keep 0.8}: one-hot encoding (48 binaries, or 72 with an explicit dense bit), an exact bytes constraint, a second-order factorization-machine (FM) surrogate of rank 4–8 on log(PPL/PPL_dense) warm-started from the ~70 rows we have, 24 real evaluations per run, and the acquisition QUBO solved by enumeration, CP-SAT, greedy, SA or SB. This note answers five questions from the primary papers: how many evaluations FMQA/BOCS-type loops need at 24–60 binaries to beat random search, local search or one-shot models (Q1); how they handle one-hot, cardinality and budget constraints (Q2); whether the acquisition solver matters (Q3); warm starts, FM size, batch diversity and failure modes (Q4); and whether such loops have been applied to network compression, quantisation or mixed precision (Q5). Every seed link was opened on arxiv.org, and the full text of the eleven core papers was read (arXiv HTML, or the PDF text extracted locally); the other sources were read at abstract or section level, as stated per citation. Numbers are copied from tables or text; a result that exists only as a curve is marked "not reported (plot only)". "FM factor size K" below is the FM's latent dimension, not an SVD rank. Two sources returned HTTP 403 and are tagged [unsure]. Nothing here is a measurement of ours; our own numbers are quoted from `docs/LOG.md` and the D1 brief, and anything derived from them is an **estimate** with its arithmetic.

## Findings

1. **Seed links: all four exist and match; one is a review.** arXiv 2507.18003 is Tamura, Seki, Minamoto, Kitai, Matsuda, Tanaka and Tsuda, "Black-box optimization using factorization and Ising machines" (v1 24 Jul 2025; Applied Physics Reviews 13, 021307, 2026). It is a review of FMQA tools and applications and contains no new head-to-head benchmark. arXiv 2507.21024 is SWIFT-FMQA (Nakano, Seki, Kikuchi, Tanaka; v2 24 Jan 2026; IEEE Access 2026); its only benchmark is the LABS problem and it has no random-search baseline. arXiv 2501.04225 is the polynomial-kernel method "kernel-QA" (Minamoto, Sakamoto; v2 6 Nov 2025), benchmarked on Rosenbrock and Rastrigin against GP Bayesian optimisation (and FMQA only at 5 real variables), with no random-search or local-search baseline. arXiv 1806.08838 is BOCS (Baptista, Poloczek; ICML 2018, PMLR 80). The original FMQA paper is Kitai et al., Phys. Rev. Research 2, 013319 (2020), whose supplement compares acquisition solvers. [verified]
2. **Q1, FMQA at 16 and 24 bits (Kitai 2020).** At 16 bits (65,536 structures, 50 random initial points, 16 runs, exhaustive search of the FM as acquisition) the FM loop found the global optimum within 300 evaluations and ranked ahead of GP Bayesian optimisation and random search (§III.A, Fig. 3a). At 24 bits with a D-Wave 2000Q acquisition, FMQA beats random search (Fig. 3b); the evaluation count at which it pulls ahead is not reported (plot only). FM factor size was fixed at K = 8 (§II.C). [verified]
3. **Q1, FMQA needs up to ~2,000 evaluations at 24 bits and more at 45 bits (Kitai 2020 and supplement).** Supplement note C: at 24 bits with 100 random initial points and 16 runs, every quantum-annealer-acquisition run reached the global optimum in under 2,000 evaluations, while 7 of 16 greedy-acquisition runs and 2 of 16 SA-acquisition runs had not after 2,000. At 16 bits (50 initial points, 20 runs) all QA and greedy runs reached it within 1,000 evaluations and 5 SA runs did not (Figs. C-1, C-2). For a 45-bit instance (L = 5, C = 8) the authors judge 2,000 evaluations too few to find the optimum (main text §III.B, Fig. 4c). [verified]
4. **Q1, BOCS at 21–25 binaries (Baptista & Poloczek 2018).** Ising sparsification, 24 binaries, 20 initial points, value after 150 iterations (170 evaluations), λ = 0: BOCS-SDP 0.11 ± 0.04, BOCS-SA 0.19 ± 0.04, SA used directly on the black box 0.21 ± 0.05, GP-EI 0.20 ± 0.04, one-flip oblivious local search 0.54 ± 0.09, point-estimate MLE-SA 1.19 ± 0.11 (Table 2); the methods are indistinguishable early and separate as samples accumulate (Fig. 3). Contamination control, 25 binaries: BOCS "typically finds the best" schedules after about 80 iterations and is best after 250 (Table 3, Fig. 4). Aero-structural coupling, 21 binaries: plain SA on the black box is best overall (Fig. 6). Random search is clearly behind on the 10-variable BQP (Figs. 1–2). [verified]
5. **Q1, at 43–60 binaries 270 evaluations were not enough for BOCS to beat SA (COMBO paper, NeurIPS 2019).** With 270 evaluations including 20 random initial points and 25 runs, on weighted MaxSAT with 28, 43 and 60 binaries (lower is better), SA used directly on the black box beat BOCS-SDP at all three sizes: −31.81 vs −29.49, −75.76 vs −51.13, −187.55 vs −153.67. Third-order BOCS-SA3 beat SA only at 28 binaries (−34.79); at 43 binaries it scored −61.02 without finishing 270 evaluations in 168 h (about 200 done), and it was not run at 60 (Table 3 and text). On 24-binary Ising (170 evaluations) BOCS-SDP beat SA at λ = 10⁻⁴ (0.059 vs 0.117) but not at λ = 0 (0.105 vs 0.095) (Table 1). [verified]
6. **Q1, BOCS and FMQA on a 24-bit matrix-compression problem (Kadowaki & Ambai 2022).** Budget per run: 24 initial points plus 2n² = 1,152 iterations (1,176 evaluations), 25 runs on each of 10 instances (100 for random search). Runs finding the exact solution (Table 1 reports counts "per 25 runs"; totals over the 10 instances): random search 9, FMQA with K = 8 66, FMQA with K = 12 76, BOCS horseshoe prior 74, BOCS normal prior 91 (SA), 104 (QA), 102 (simulated quenching). FMQA improves fastest early and then stalls; BOCS improves slowly but keeps improving (Fig. 1). [verified]
7. **Q1, FM vs BOCS with a cardinality target (Matsumori, Taki, Kadowaki 2022).** PCB mounting holes, 1,800 evaluations per run, 20 runs. At 17 binaries (weighted-sum objective) success ratios were 80 % (FM) and 55 % (BOCS) at 1,800 evaluations (Fig. 5). At 17 binaries with exactly 6 holes (12,376 feasible) both exceeded 80 % (Fig. 7b). At 27 binaries with exactly 8 holes (2,220,075 feasible) no difference between FM and BOCS could be identified (Fig. 8b). Random search was worse wherever the text compares it (Figs. 4, 7a); the gaps are plots only. [verified]
8. **Q1, a 252-state constrained problem needs ~50 evaluations (Nawa et al. 2023).** Magnetic tunnel junction, 10 sites with exactly 5 of each ion (252 feasible), 20 initial points, 10 trials: FM + annealer found the best ΔE_total structure within about 50 evaluations including the 20 initial ones, while random search needed more than half of the 252; for the near-degenerate resistance-area target Bayesian optimisation beat every FM variant (§III.B, Figs. 3–4). [verified]
9. **Q1, SWIFT-FMQA on LABS: 22,600 black-box calls do not find the optimum at 64 bits.** Each iteration samples 15 solutions from the annealer, calls the black box on all 15 (Step 3) and adds the best 3 to the training set (Step 4). With 100 initial points and 1,500 iterations (Table 1) that is 100 + 1,500 × 15 = 22,600 calls and 4,600 training points per run (our arithmetic), 50 runs. At N = 16 plain FMQA found the optimum in 1 of 50 runs; SWIFT-FMQA with windows of 150–400 points found it in 40–46 of 50 (App. A, Table 2). At N = 64 neither method found it in any run at any window size (§V-A, Fig. 3), and at N = 49 it was not found in the setting examined (App. A). [verified]
10. **Q1, subsampling FM annealing at 20 bits (Hama & Kadowaki, arXiv 2508.08778).** Matrix-compression benchmark, 2n² + 1 = 801 iterations, 30 trials on each of 10 instances: runs ending at the optimum were 11/300 for standardised FMA, 144/300 for standardised subsampled FMA (SFMA) and 2/300 for random search; without output standardisation both FMA and SFMA scored 0/300 (Table II). [verified]
11. **Q1, kernel-QA (arXiv 2501.04225).** With 10 random initial points and 10 runs, on 40-binary Rastrigin kernel-QA reached 0.0 against 1.9 for GP-EI at the end of the plotted cycles (§4.2.2, Fig. 6). At 5 real variables after 1,000 cycles FMQA was the worst of three on Rosenbrock (13.4, against 0.7 for kernel-QA and 4.9 for GP-EI) and comparable on Rastrigin (FMQA 1.3, kernel-QA 1.4, GP-EI 3.1) (§4.1, Fig. 3). No random-search or local-search baseline is reported. [verified]
12. **Q1, the smallest budgets in the literature are not budget-matched.** Wing shape, 100 one-hot bits: 20 random + 20 FMQA evaluations gave a 17 % better lift-to-drag ratio than 20 random evaluations, a comparison at 40 vs 20 evaluations (review §IV.C.2). Traffic signals, 960 one-hot bits: 10 random + 90 FMQA evaluations, no baseline (review §IV.C.3). Car bodies (222 real variables, 42 black-box constraints): 3 evaluations per cycle × 300 cycles = 900, reported, in ambiguous wording, as improving on NSGA-II with 30,000 evaluations (review §IV.C.4; primary, Kondo et al. 2025, not opened). A one-shot baseline (surrogate fitted once, never refitted) is not reported in any paper read; the nearest is BOCS's point-estimate variant, which is refitted but never explores and plateaus (findings 4, 26). [verified]
13. **Q1, the rule of thumb is O(n²) evaluations.** A quadratic surrogate has O(n²) coefficients and so needs O(n²) data (Koshikawa et al. §2.1); Kadowaki & Ambai and Hama & Kadowaki budget 2n² iterations. **Estimate**: 2 × 48² = 4,608 evaluations at 48 binaries and 2 × 72² = 10,368 at 72; D1's 24 evaluations per run is 0.5 % of the former. [inferred]
14. **Q2, constraints are almost always quadratic penalties in FMQA.** One-hot α(Σx − 1)² (review Eq. 15), domain-wall (Eq. 17), cardinality α(Σx − N/2)² and α(Σx − N_c)² (Eqs. 19–21), three one-hot groups at once (Eq. 23). If α is too small the proposals violate the constraint; if too large, better solutions are lost; α = 1 was used for the MTJ design (review §IV.B.1; Nawa §III.B). The PCB study uses F(x) = −p f̃ + (N − N̄)² with p = 1/450 (Matsumori Eq. 9). [verified]
15. **Q2, "hard" constraints in the Fixstars tools are penalties underneath.** Amplify SDK and Amplify-BBOpt accept hard one-hot, domain-wall, equality and inequality constraints, and the SDK performs "penalization of constraints" before handing the problem to a solver (review §II.E.2, §III.2). [verified]
16. **Q2, feasibility-preserving search and exact constrained solves are the alternatives.** BOCS fits its model on one-hot-expanded categorical variables but runs SA on the categorical tuples themselves, whose neighbours change one variable's category, so every proposal is feasible (BOCS Supp. A). EvoPress keeps the global compression level fixed with a level-switch mutation that raises one unit's level and lowers another's (EvoPress §3). CoopQ solves its layer-wise binary quadratic program exactly as a MILP in SCIP with a hard memory inequality Σ c_i(1 − q_i) ≤ B (arXiv 2509.15455). BOCS's own experiments use an ℓ1 term λ‖x‖₁, a soft budget, not a hard one (BOCS §3.2). [verified]
17. **Q2, repair and infeasible data.** Kitai et al. replace a proposal that is already in the dataset by a random unevaluated structure (§II.D). Matsumori et al. add the QUBO optimum plus two random 1–2-bit-flip neighbours per iteration even when the neighbours break the cardinality constraint (in the worst case only a third of the data are feasible) and argue that the infeasible points help fit the penalised objective (§2.2, §3). [verified]
18. **Q2, encoding and initial data.** For integer variables, one-hot encoding in the learning stage is the dominant factor, ahead of domain-wall or binary (Ogawa et al., arXiv 2606.23188); one-hot and domain-wall beat binary and unary on RNA design (Kikuchi & Tanaka, arXiv 2602.16643); one-hot suits small problems (Seki, Tamura, Tanaka, arXiv 2209.01016). With one-hot encoding and uniform random initial data some bits are never 1, so their FM parameters get no gradient; Latin-hypercube or Sobol' initial designs that set every bit at least once did better (Hayashi et al., arXiv 2605.04825). Function-smoothing regularisation helps FMs on one-hot-encoded continuous variables (review §II.D.1, citing Endo & Takahashi 2025). [verified]
19. **Q3, no acquisition-solver effect at 24 bits (Kadowaki & Ambai 2022).** Normal-prior BOCS with SA, D-Wave QA or simulated quenching (temperature held at 0.1, close to greedy descent) showed no clear differences (Fig. 2; exact-solution counts 91, 104 and 102, Table 1). The authors conclude that the surrogate's landscape is simple, that a simple Ising solver is enough, and that the advantage of QA in black-box optimisation is an open question (Discussion). [verified]
20. **Q3, the same holds in three other studies.** On a 20-spin SK black box (10 initial points, 50 instances × 10 runs), BOCS with D-Wave 2000Q and with SA were indistinguishable and both beat BOCS with SDP relaxation plus rounding (Koshikawa et al. §3, Fig. 2). In the MTJ study an exact solver on the FM, QA and SA were comparable for ΔE_total and TMR, and SA beat QA on average for RA (Nawa §III.B, Fig. 4). BOCS-SDP and BOCS-SA are close in all BOCS tables, with SDP ahead at 24 binaries after 150 iterations (0.11 vs 0.19, Table 2). The PCB study saw a slightly higher success ratio with QA than SA and declined to call QA better (Matsumori §3, Fig. 9). [verified]
21. **Q3, the one counter-example is about proposal diversity, not solution quality.** In Kitai's supplement, QA reached the optimum in fewer evaluations than SA and random-start greedy acquisition because SA repeatedly re-selected the same local-optimum structure (note C). kernel-QA had to flip half the bits of its binary test functions because Amplify AE starts its search near x = 0, which would otherwise favour test functions whose optimum lies at 0 (§4.2.2). None of the papers read uses simulated bifurcation as the acquisition solver. [verified]
22. **Q3, inference for D1.** On a 48–72-binary surrogate QUBO with one linear equality, an exact solve (CP-SAT or MIQP) or greedy descent should return the surrogate optimum; annealers help a closed loop only as an unplanned source of randomness, which D1 should supply explicitly (findings 26–27). This matches our own result that greedy descent beat SB and SA on the planner QUBOs. [inferred]
23. **Q4, FM factor size K.** Kitai et al. fixed K = 8 (the libFM default) and, in supplement note B, found that fewer factors predict better when training data are few (the note puts the threshold near 200 points) (§II.C, Fig. B-1). The MTJ study tuned K = 7, 2 and 3 for its three targets (Nawa Table II). The PCB study found k = 12 or 15 best among {9, 12, 15}, depending on the QUBO formulation, and notes that the original FMQA paper saw no k dependence (Matsumori §4.1.1, Fig. 10). SWIFT-FMQA's gain held for K = 4, 6, 10 and 16 (Fig. 4b). One car-body study trained a deliberately over-fitted large-K FM, an under-fitted small-K FM and their linear mix each cycle and evaluated all three proposals (review §IV.C.4). [verified]
24. **Q4, D1's FM is under-determined without a prior.** **Estimate** from the FM parameter count NK + N + 1 (review §II.B): N = 48, K = 4 gives 241 parameters; N = 48, K = 8 gives 433; N = 72, K = 8 gives 649; D1 has about 70 warm-start rows plus 24 per run. A full quadratic (BOCS) at N = 48 has 1 + 48 + 1,128 = 1,177 coefficients. [inferred]
25. **Q4, warm starts.** SWIFT-FMQA initialises the FM parameters once and carries them from iteration to iteration (§IV-B). Seki, Nakada and Tanaka (arXiv 2410.12747 v3) initialise an FM by a low-rank approximation of a known approximate Ising model, reproduce it more accurately than random initialisation (Fig. 2), propose this for warm-starting FMQA, and give a predicted rank from an eigenvalue threshold (§IV–V). [verified]
26. **Q4, stagnation and exploration.** As the dataset grows, each new point's share of the loss shrinks and FMQA stalls; training only on the latest 10–300 points beat FMQA at N = 64, best near 100 (SWIFT §III, Fig. 3a). BOCS explores by sampling the regression coefficients from the posterior (Thompson sampling); with a point estimate it is purely exploitative and plateaus (Table 2: MLE-SA 1.19 vs BOCS-SA 0.19; Supp. E). FMQA's point-estimated FM is deterministic and stays in a local minimum once trapped (Kadowaki & Ambai, Discussion; Hama & Kadowaki). Reported remedies: training each FM on a random subset of the data (finding 10: 11 → 144 of 300), adding random neighbours of the optimum (the variant without them reached the optimum only once in 20 runs, Matsumori Fig. 9), forbidding duplicate samples, which "significantly reduces" the steps to the optimum, most of all with a maximum-a-posteriori acquisition (Morita, Nishikawa, Ohzeki, arXiv 2309.02842), and evaluating 15 annealer samples per iteration and training on the best 3 (SWIFT Table 1, §II-D–E). The review states that no definitive batch method for FMQA exists yet (§V). [verified]
27. **Q4, batch acquisition by reverse annealing (FMIRA).** A 2025 package, FMIRA, updates several particles by reverse annealing on local FMs and is reported to beat FMQA's noise-based batch sampling; the article returned HTTP 403, so this rests on search-result text only. [unsure]
28. **Q4, quadratic surrogates fail when the objective has strong higher-order terms.** BOCS and FMQA fit the cost locally, not globally (Kadowaki & Ambai, Discussion). On the 24-edge Ising benchmark, 167 of 2,048 third-order coefficients exceed 0.1 with 100 samples, and third-order BOCS does better after 150 iterations (BOCS Supp. B, Figs. 9–10). Third-order BOCS at 43 binaries has 13,288 coefficients, and with higher-order terms BOCS "suffers severely" from inefficient training because there are far more parameters than samples (COMBO §4.3). The review names higher-order (HUBO) surrogates as future work (§V). LABS, the SWIFT benchmark, is quartic and was never solved at N ≥ 49 (finding 9). [verified]
29. **Q4, negative results.** FMQA did not beat Bayesian optimisation or a genetic algorithm on 15-bit polymer design (review §IV.A.3). SA on the black box beat BOCS at 21 binaries (BOCS Fig. 6) and at 43–60 binaries (finding 5). Bayesian optimisation beat all FM variants on a near-degenerate target (finding 8). FMQA was the worst of three on Rosenbrock at 5 real variables (finding 11). [verified]
30. **Q4, output scaling and noise.** Standardising the outputs was the difference between 0/300 and 11–144/300 successes (Hama & Kadowaki Table II); the MTJ study min-max normalised its targets and fitted RA on a log scale (Nawa Table II); kernel-QA applies an exponential output transform (§2, Eq. 16). BOCS models Gaussian observation noise (§2), and EvoPress scores candidates on progressively more samples to cut fitness noise (§3). A noisy black box is not studied in any FMQA paper read (not reported). [verified]
31. **Q5, the only FMQA/BOCS use on network weights is a 24-bit toy (Kadowaki & Ambai 2022).** They decompose a matrix into binary × real factors: the 4,096 × 1,000 final fully connected layer of VGG16 is shrunk by SVD to 8 × 100, and with K = 3 this gives 24 binary variables (Results, Methods). The authors state that with the current scaling the method does not apply to real machine-learning weight matrices (Discussion). It decides factor entries, not per-layer ranks or bits. [verified]
32. **Q5, the FMQA review lists no pruning, quantisation, low-rank or mixed-precision application.** Its machine-learning uses are feature selection and matrix compression (§IV.A intro, §IV.A.5); hyperparameter tuning is named only as a use case (abstract, §I). [verified]
33. **Q5, FMQA for hyperparameter optimisation.** A 2024 geomechanics paper (Xiao et al., doi 10.1002/nag.3800) tunes the hyperparameters of a Gaussian-process metamodel with FMQA and reports it competitive with Bayesian optimisation; the article returned HTTP 403, so this rests on search-result text only. [unsure]
34. **Q5, closed-loop search over per-layer LLM compression is published, with other surrogates.** Bolaco (Ji et al., EMNLP Findings 2024, arXiv 2405.10616) runs GP + EI Bayesian optimisation for 50 iterations over 5 or 20 grouped low-rank dimensions of LLaMA-2 7B/13B at 20–30 % compression, because the effect of per-layer ranks "is combinatorial" and optimising components independently may give a local optimum (§3.2, §4.2). AMQ (Lee et al., EMNLP 2025, arXiv 2509.12019) chooses 2, 3 or 4 bits per linear layer with an RBF quality predictor (JSD to the original logits) and NSGA-II, evaluating 10,250 configurations directly on Llama 2 7B while predicting over 800,000 (§3.1, §3.4–3.5, §5.1; RBF and MLP predictors differ little, App. E Table 9). EvoPress (Sieberling et al., ICML 2025, arXiv 2410.14649) searches per-block and per-layer levels (depth, sparsity, bit-width) by evolution with direct KL fitness, and shows that removing strictly more blocks can lower perplexity (§1 Table 1, §3). [verified]
35. **Q5, one-shot measured-interaction QUBOs for LLM mixed precision are also published.** CoopQ (formerly IMPQ; Zhao et al., arXiv 2509.15455 v2) estimates layer sensitivities and pairwise interactions by Shapley sampling (about 18 h on one A40 for 100 samples on Llama-3.1-8B) and solves the 2/4-bit allocation exactly under a hard memory budget, on Gemma-2, Llama-3 and Qwen3 (2B–9B). CLADO and CASA are already cited in `docs/PROPOSAL.md`. One-shot QUBOs for pruning or quantising CNNs and small dense networks, solved by annealers or SA, also exist (arXiv 2505.16332, 2604.05856, 2609.22238, 2510.16075); from their abstracts, none refits a surrogate from new measurements (2609.22238 refines its QUBO allocation by direct validation-based search). [verified]
36. **Q5, not found.** In more than 20 searches we found no loop that uses an FM or BOCS quadratic surrogate with a QUBO acquisition over per-layer choices (rank or bits) for LLM compression. This rests on the searches finding nothing, not on proof. [unsure]

## Contradictions with our results

- **Solver: consistent, with one caveat for the loop.** Our one-shot finding that greedy descent beats SB and SA matches findings 19–20: at 10–24 binaries an exact solver, a near-greedy quench, SA and QA give the same loop results. The exception (finding 21) is a closed-loop effect: a deterministic acquisition can propose the same plan again and again. A D1 loop with an exact or greedy solver therefore needs an explicit rule against duplicates and a source of exploration, or it will stall like point-estimate BOCS (finding 26).
- **Budget: D1's expectation is out of line with every reported success.** Successes at 10–27 binaries used 50–2,000 evaluations (findings 2–8); at 43–64 binaries 270 evaluations (BOCS against SA), 2,000 (FMQA at 45 bits) and 22,600 black-box calls (SWIFT-FMQA at 64 bits) were all reported as insufficient (findings 3, 5, 9). Twenty-four evaluations per run can test whether the loop improves on the one-shot pairwise plan at matched bytes, but not whether it finds the optimum or beats local search on the real objective.
- **Super-additivity: our data show why a quadratic model underpredicts large plans, as the literature warns (finding 28).** **Estimate**, using the D1 brief (24 MLPs at keep 0.8: singles sum +58 %, together +276 %; 40 pairs: median interaction +0.21 pp) and assuming equal singles of 58/24 = 2.42 % each: in log space the singles give 24 × ln(1.0242) = 0.573; a median pair adds ln(1.0504) − 2 ln(1.0242) = 0.00145, so all C(24, 2) = 276 pairs add 0.399; the total 0.972 predicts +164 % against the measured ln(3.76) = 1.324 (+276 %). Median-sized pair terms thus explain 0.399 / 0.751 = 53 % of the log-space excess over the singles (in linear space 58 + 276 × 0.21 = +116 %, 27 % of the excess). The rest needs larger pair terms, third-order terms or a plan-size term.
- **Individual pair terms are poorly identified, so plan-scale rows must carry the fit.** **Estimate**: at the 24-MLP plan's mean single cost of 2.42 %, the product model's pair term is 2.42²/100 = 0.058 pp, about a quarter of the measured median of 0.21 pp, and that median is below both pair models' leave-one-pair-out mean error (0.51–0.56 pp). Single pairs therefore identify individual couplings poorly, while an 18–24-MLP plan aggregates up to 276 of them. This agrees with the local-fit argument of Kadowaki & Ambai (finding 28): a loop that samples near the budget slice can correct the plan-scale bias that singles and pairs cannot. [inferred]
- **Warm-start data are off-distribution.** Our ~70 rows are mostly single layers and pairs, while the acquisition will propose 18–24-MLP plans. Every FMQA paper read warm-starts from uniformly random configurations, so none tests this shift; the one-hot coverage result (finding 18) at least requires each (layer, keep) bit to appear in some multi-layer row. [inferred]
- **A uniform positive coupling does not always move the argmin.** Under an exact bytes constraint, a pair term that is the same for every pair adds a constant when all feasible plans compress the same number of MLPs; it changes the plan only when the byte target can be met with different numbers of compressed MLPs (mixing keep 0.9 and 0.8), and then it favours fewer, more heavily compressed MLPs. ## Recommendation

- **Evaluation budget:** treat 24 evaluations per run as a local correction of the one-shot plan, judged against budget-matched baselines (random feasible plans and the top-k distinct plans of the current pairwise model); any claim of beating local search or finding the optimum at 48–72 binaries needs hundreds to thousands of evaluations (50–2,000 used at 10–27 binaries; 270, 2,000 and 22,600 reported insufficient at 43–64).
- **Surrogate form:** a second-order FM on standardised log(PPL/PPL_dense) with K = 4 (at most 8), initialised from the current pairwise QUBO by low-rank approximation (arXiv 2410.12747), weighted towards plan-scale rows, with exploration from the model (posterior sampling as in BOCS, or subsample training as in SFMA), and a third-order or plan-size term added if held-out plan residuals stay positive.
- **Acquisition solver:** solve the surrogate exactly with CP-SAT/MIQP (or greedy) under the bytes equality, return the top-k distinct feasible plans and reject anything already measured; SA and QA added nothing on surrogate QUBOs this small (Kadowaki & Ambai, Koshikawa et al., Nawa et al.), no paper read uses SB, and our own runs had greedy beat SB and SA.
- **Constraint handling:** one-hot per MLP for learning, with one-hot and the exact bytes budget as hard constraints in the exact solver (as CoopQ does with SCIP, or a level-switch move as in EvoPress) instead of α-penalties, and a warm-start set that turns on every (layer, keep) bit in at least one multi-layer plan.
- **Novelty verdict: replication.** The closed loop is published for per-layer LLM compression (Bolaco 2024, GP-BO over LLaMA-2 low-rank allocation; AMQ 2025, RBF + NSGA-II over 2/3/4 bits); the pairwise QUBO is published (CLADO, CoopQ, CASA); FMQA/BOCS on network weights exists only as a 24-bit toy (Kadowaki & Ambai 2022); the FM + QUBO pairing for per-layer LLM compression was not found, which makes D1 a method transfer, not a new result.
