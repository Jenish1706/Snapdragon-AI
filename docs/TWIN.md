# SnapTwin: llama.cpp performance model and held-out error

Generated by `scripts/report_twin.py` from `results/twin/*.csv`; do not edit by hand.

## Model

Per device and compute unit, SnapTwin predicts llama.cpp decode and prefill time from per-token model facts (weight bytes W, KV bytes K, matmul FLOPs F, attention FLOPs per query-key pair A, read from the GGUF headers):

```
decode per token at mean depth d:  t = a_w * W + a_kv * K * d + t0
prefill of n tokens:               t = b_f * F * n + b_attn * A * n(n+1)/2 + b0
```

Coefficients are non-negative least squares on relative error. 1/a_w is the unit's effective weight bandwidth. To predict another chip the fitted efficiencies and overheads are kept, the bandwidth terms are rescaled by nominal bandwidth, and the compute terms by a compute ratio (1 unless stated). Every transferred number is a prediction.

### What the held-out numbers say

- Decode transfers by bandwidth on CPU and GPU: median error 21 % over all phone-to-PC cells, because those paths are bandwidth-bound.
- NPU decode does not: median error 58 %. It is dominated by per-token overheads that differ between Hexagon generations and between the phone and PC drivers.
- Prefill does not transfer with compute ratio 1: CPU/GPU median error 61 %, NPU 47 %. PC prefill needs a compute ratio (core count, clocks, HMX width) that SnapTwin cannot infer from bandwidth.
- For the X2 Elite CRD, 152 GB/s fits the published decode better than 228 GB/s (see the rows above), consistent with a non-Extreme part, although Qualcomm does not state the CRD's SKU.
