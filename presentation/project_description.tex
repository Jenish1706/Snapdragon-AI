\paragraph{Executive summary.} Ising-Edge chooses, for every transformer layer, whether and how strongly to compress it under an exact memory budget. It measures perplexity, fits a quadratic (QUBO) surrogate, and solves it exactly with CP-SAT \citep{ortools}. On Qwen3-0.6B, model-chosen plans beat the best of three random plans at equal bytes by \V{mb.K18.abs}\,pp of $\Delta$PPL at $K{=}18$ (95\% CI [\V{mb.K18.lo}, \V{mb.K18.hi}]), but lose at $K{=}24$. The same QUBO form includes Snapdragon CPU/GPU/NPU placement and latency terms; that part is implemented but has \emph{not} been validated on a device in this repository.

\paragraph{Problem and motivation.} Edge devices constrain the memory and bandwidth available to small language models. Low-rank compression saves bytes, but its cost depends on which layers are compressed: compressing one MLP at keep 0.8 costs from +\V{sens.k08.min}\% (layer \V{sens.k08.min.layer}) to +\V{sens.k08.max}\% (layer \V{sens.k08.max.layer}) perplexity (median +\V{sens.k08.median}\%). Costs also compound: a 24-MLP plan measured +\V{mb.K24.plan.fast}\% against +\V{mb.K24.pred.lin}\% from summing single-layer costs (\V{mb.K24.ratio}$\times$); \V{pairs.super} of \V{pairs.n} measured layer pairs are super-additive. A plan must therefore be chosen jointly over layers, in a space of \V{loop.K12.count} feasible plans for one byte budget.

\paragraph{Approach.} (1) \emph{Measure}: an oracle compresses a plan's MLPs (gate, up, down) with activation-aware truncated SVD (keep 0.8 = rank \V{rank.k08}, keep 0.9 = rank \V{rank.k09}) and scores WikiText-2 \citep{merity2017wikitext} perplexity (context \V{ctx}, 40 fixed chunks), keeping per-chunk NLL. (2) \emph{Fit}: a quadratic surrogate of $\log(\mathrm{PPL}/\mathrm{PPL}_{\text{dense}})$ over \V{loop.nbin} one-hot plan bits, either a pair-interaction model selected by leave-one-pair-out error (one-shot) or a rank-\V{loop.fm.rank} factorization machine \citep{rendle2010fm} refitted every round (closed loop, in the spirit of FMQA/BOCS \citep{kitai2020fmqa,baptista2018bocs}). (3) \emph{Solve}: CP-SAT minimises the surrogate with at most one option per layer, an exact byte budget and no-good cuts on measured plans; each round evaluates the optimum plus a Hamming-diverse pick. Chosen plans are re-measured on the full \V{dense.full.chunks}-chunk split with paired-bootstrap CIs (\V{mb.boot.reps} resamples).

\paragraph{Implementation.} Python package \texttt{isingedge}: \texttt{loop/} (space, FM, acquisition, oracle, statistics; evaluated), \texttt{twin/} (SnapTwin prefill/decode roofline model fitted by NNLS; unit-tested), \texttt{qubo/} (placement QUBO over CPU/GPU/NPU with unit-switch costs; unit-tested), \texttt{bench/} and \texttt{scripts/} (adb + llama.cpp device runners; not yet run). All results were measured with \V{env.build}. The test suite collects 56 tests: 52 pass, 3 are skipped without optional torch/onnxruntime, and 1 smoke test fails because two subpackages are missing from this snapshot.

\paragraph{Impact and use case.} Given a byte budget, the planner returns per-layer keep fractions whose accuracy has been measured, with confidence intervals, rather than predicted from single-layer tables. The target runtime is llama.cpp \citep{llamacpp} on Snapdragon; Qwen3 (Apache-2.0) and llama.cpp (MIT) keep the stack open source.

\paragraph{Limitations.} No Snapdragon latency, memory or energy measurement exists in this repository; SnapTwin and the placement QUBO are unit-tested only, and the plan-export scripts reference data not included here. Results cover one model, one metric (WikiText-2 perplexity) and one runtime (PyTorch fp32), without task accuracy or healing; damage is large (+\V{mb.K18.plan.full}\% at $K{=}18$). The pair model fails for large plans ($K{=}24$), and the loop's gains are small at 24 evaluations. The components are established techniques; the contribution is their integration with an exact byte budget and measured validation.

\paragraph{Future work.} Run the scripted llama-bench sweeps on a Snapdragon device (CPU, GPU, NPU); fit SnapTwin on device data and add latency terms; export plans as llama.cpp tensor overrides and measure time-to-first-token, decode rate and memory; add plan-size terms, task accuracy and healing.
